# Episode 1 — The Moonflower Seed
*A Semente da Flor da Lua*

## A. Episode Information

- **Episode number:** 1
- **English title:** The Moonflower Seed
- **Portuguese title:** A Semente da Flor da Lua
- **Main theme:** Hidden magic, patience and caring for something small
- **Moral lesson:** The most wonderful things often need a little care and a lot of patience.
- **Estimated duration:** 6:54 (13 scenes, narration 787 words)
- **Setting:** The Whispering Meadow and Whispering Hill at night, under a sky full of shooting stars
- **Characters:** Tuca, Pipoca, Glimmers, Pedrão

**Synopsis:** During a shower of shooting stars, a glowing seed lands at Tuca's feet. Its light is fading. Guided by tiny glowing moon moths called Glimmers, Tuca and Pipoca carry it across a sleeping meadow, over a sparkling stream and even over a snoring Pedrão to reach the top of Whispering Hill. They plant it and wait... and wait... until their patience is rewarded with a giant Moonflower that lights the whole valley.

## B. Complete Story and Narration

*(Single narrator. Ready for voice recording.)*

On the night the sky sparkled, something tiny fell from the stars... and landed right at Tuca's feet. It glowed like a little moon. But to understand how it got there, we have to go back just a few moments, to a quiet hill at the edge of the Whispering Meadow.

Tuca and his best friend, Pipoca, were lying in the soft grass, counting shooting stars. One, two, three... Pipoca tried to count too, but it kept getting so excited that it rolled over and forgot what came after three. The sky was full of silver streaks, like someone was drawing on it with light.

Then one little star did not fade away. It fell lower, and lower, and lower... and plop! There it was, glowing in the grass. Tuca picked it up very gently. It was not a star at all. It was a seed, smooth and silver, warm like a sleepy kitten. But as they watched, its light began to flicker, growing weaker and weaker, like a candle on a windy night.

Just then, a soft flutter came from the tall grass. One by one, tiny glowing moths floated out, like little lanterns with wings. They were Glimmers, the shy moon moths of the meadow. They circled the seed, and then, all together, they pointed their golden antennae toward the tallest hill in the valley, where the moon was shining brightest.

Tuca understood. The seed needed the moonlight on top of Whispering Hill, and it needed it before the moon went down. So Tuca held the seed close to his heart, Pipoca puffed out its chest bravely, and off they went across the sleeping meadow, where the flowers had closed their petals and even the wind was whispering.

Soon they reached a sparkling stream. The water was too wide to jump across. But the Glimmers flew low and lit up a row of round, flat stones. Hop. Hop. Hop. Tuca went first. Pipoca followed, wobbling on its chubby legs, until its eggshell hat almost slipped off. Tuca reached back, caught the hat, and set it gently on Pipoca's head again.

On the other side, the grass grew taller than Tuca. Everything was shadowy and rustly, and Pipoca pressed close to Tuca's leg. Tuca was a little nervous too. So he did what his mother always did when he was scared. He hummed a soft, slow song. And the Glimmers, who loved gentle sounds, glowed brighter and brighter, until the whole path shone like a little river of light.

At the bottom of the hill, the path was blocked by a big, round, snoring mountain. Except it was not a mountain at all. It was Pedrão, the sleepy ankylosaurus, fast asleep right across the trail. Zzzz... Tuca and Pipoca pushed, but Pedrão did not move one bit. He just smiled in his sleep and snored a little louder.

Then Tuca had an idea. If they could not go around him, they could go over him! Very, very quietly, they climbed up Pedrão's bumpy back, step by careful step, like climbing a cozy, breathing hill. Pedrão only mumbled, smiled, and kept on dreaming. And on the other side, Whispering Hill was waiting.

At the very top of the hill, the moonlight was so bright that their shadows looked painted on the grass. The seed was barely glowing now. Tuca knelt down and dug a little hole with his hands. Pipoca helped, digging with its tiny feet and tossing dirt everywhere, mostly on Tuca. Together, they laid the seed inside and covered it with the softest soil they could find.

Then they waited. And waited. Pipoca tapped its little foot. It peeked at the soil. It peeked again. Nothing. Pipoca huffed and flopped down with a sad little sigh. But Tuca sat beside the tiny mound and smiled. Some things, he thought, cannot be rushed. So they watched the moon together, and the Glimmers settled around them like a blanket of stars.

Just as the moon touched the edge of the hill, the soil began to shimmer. A tiny silver sprout poked out. It stretched, and grew, and twirled, higher and higher, until a giant flower opened above them, as big as a tree, glowing with soft blue light. Its petals sparkled, and from its center, thousands of tiny lights floated up into the sky, becoming stars once again.

Tuca and Pipoca fell asleep that night under the glowing Moonflower, cozy and proud. And from then on, every evening, when the stars came out, the Moonflower lit up Whispering Hill like a night-light for the whole valley. Because the most wonderful things often need just a little care... and a lot of patience.
