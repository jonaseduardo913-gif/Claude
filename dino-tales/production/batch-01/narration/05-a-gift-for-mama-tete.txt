A Gift for Mama Tetê

Pipoca woke up before the sun, before the birds, and even before the flowers had opened their eyes. Because today was the most important day of the whole year. It was Mama Tetê's special day. Every day, Mama Tetê did so many kind things for Pipoca. She kept it warm, she hummed it to sleep, and she always saved it the sweetest berries. Now it was Pipoca's turn. And Pipoca had a big, secret plan.

Pipoca hurried to Tuca's cave and nudged him awake. Tuca rubbed his sleepy eyes. Pipoca jumped, pointed with its nose toward the meadow, and made a big circle shape with its tail. Tuca smiled. He understood. They were going to make the most perfect present in the whole world.

In the flower meadow, they picked the brightest blossoms they could find. Red ones, yellow ones, purple ones, and one very special pink flower, the same color as Mama Tetê's bow. Tuca wove them carefully into a big flower crown, just the right size for a mother triceratops.

Next, they went down to the river, where the pebbles were smooth and shiny. They picked the prettiest ones, white and blue and speckled, and stacked them into a tall, sparkly tower, taller than Pipoca. Pipoca put the very last pebble on top, holding its breath. It wobbled... but it stayed! It was perfect.

But while they were celebrating, the sky grew darker. A big gray cloud rolled over the meadow, and the wind began to blow. Pitter... patter... pitter-patter... And then, all at once, it started to rain.

Tuca and Pipoca ran under a big leaf to hide. But the rain did not stop. The river rose, and with a splash, it knocked over the pebble tower. The wind picked up the flower crown and spun it into the water, where it floated away, petal by petal. Pipoca watched it go, and its eyes filled with tears.

When the rain finally stopped, everything was wet and muddy. There was no flower crown. There was no pebble tower. Pipoca sat down in the mud, very small and very sad. It had wanted to give its mother something perfect. Now there was nothing left to give. It looked at the empty riverbank, then at its muddy feet, and let out a tiny, wobbly sigh.

Then Tuca noticed something. Right next to Pipoca, pressed into the soft mud, was a perfect little footprint. Pipoca's footprint. Tuca's eyes lit up. He grabbed a big, flat piece of soft clay from the riverbank and set it down in front of Pipoca. Then he pointed at Pipoca's foot.

Pipoca understood. Very carefully, it pressed its little foot into the clay. Squish! A tiny, perfect footprint. Then Tuca pressed his hand right beside it. They picked up the rain-washed petals that had landed on the riverbank and pressed them all around the edges, pink and yellow and purple, like a little garden. It looked like a picture of their friendship, frozen in clay.

When they looked up, a rainbow had appeared over the meadow, bright and shining, as if the sky wanted to help too. Pipoca held the little clay plate very carefully. It was not shiny. It was not tall. It was not perfect. But Pipoca thought it was the most beautiful thing it had ever made. On the way home, Pipoca walked very slowly, holding the plate as if it were made of sunshine.

Back home, Mama Tetê was looking everywhere for her little one. When Pipoca walked up, muddy from nose to tail, she gasped. Then Pipoca set the clay plate down in front of her. Mama Tetê lowered her big head and looked at it for a long, long time. A tiny footprint, a little handprint, and a ring of flowers.

Then a big, happy tear rolled down her cheek. She wrapped Pipoca in the warmest, gentlest hug, and even pulled Tuca in too. Pipoca had worried that the gift was not perfect. But Mama Tetê did not need perfect. She needed something made with love. And that is exactly what she got.

From that day on, the little clay plate sat in the most special spot in the nest, where the morning sun could shine on it every day. And every time Mama Tetê looked at it, she smiled. Sometimes Pipoca looked at it too, and remembered the gray, rainy day when something beautiful had been made anyway. Because the best gifts are not perfect things. They are the ones made with love.
