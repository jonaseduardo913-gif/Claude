Pipoca's First Snow

One morning, Pipoca opened its eyes, stepped outside the cave... and froze. Everything was gone! The grass, the flowers, the path. Everything had turned white, soft and sparkly, as if the whole world had been wrapped in a giant fluffy blanket.

But Tuca ran out laughing and jumped right into it. Poof! Snow flew everywhere. It is snow, Pipoca, he seemed to say with his big happy smile. Pipoca was not so sure. It touched the snow with one toe... It was cold! Very cold! Pipoca jumped back and hid behind Tuca's legs.

Tuca knelt down and showed Pipoca something wonderful. He pressed his foot into the snow and lifted it up. There was a footprint! Pipoca tried too, very carefully. Squish. A tiny dinosaur footprint! Then a snowflake landed on Tuca's tongue, and he giggled. Pipoca opened its mouth wide and waited... and waited... until one landed right on its nose. Then they made a whole trail of footprints, big ones and little ones, side by side, all the way around the old pine tree.

Pipoca was starting to like this cold, sparkly stuff. At the top of a little hill, Tuca lay on his tummy and whooshed down. Pipoca took a deep breath, closed its eyes, and slid down after him. Faster and faster! Its eggshell hat flew up, and Pipoca landed in a soft pile of snow, laughing so hard its tail wiggled all by itself. They climbed back up and slid down again, and again, and again, until the hill had two long, shiny tracks.

Then, in the middle of all the laughter, they heard something else. A tiny sniffle. And another. It was coming from an old hollow log at the edge of the field. Tuca and Pipoca looked at each other, and very quietly, they tiptoed closer.

Inside the log, curled up like a little round stone, was a baby ankylosaurus. His name was Pebble, and he was shivering. Pebble had never seen snow before either. He was too scared to step out into all that cold whiteness, and his home was all the way across the snowy field. He had wandered out to look for berries, and when the snow began to fall, he had hidden in the log and waited, hoping the cold white stuff would go away.

Pipoca looked at Pebble and remembered how it had felt that very morning, frozen at the cave door. So Pipoca crawled into the log, sat right next to Pebble, and gently nudged him with its nose. It was the kind of nudge that means: I was scared too.

Then Pipoca stepped out into the snow and pressed its foot down. Squish. A little footprint. Pebble leaned out to look. Pipoca made another. And another. Pebble took a deep breath, stretched out one tiny foot... and squish! He made his very first footprint. His eyes went wide, and then, slowly, he smiled.

Together, the three friends set off across the snowy field. Pipoca showed Pebble how to catch snowflakes on his nose, and Tuca showed them both how to make snow angels by waving their arms and legs. Pebble's snow angel looked more like a snow potato, but he loved it anyway. And every few steps, Pebble stopped to look back at his own little footprints, and every time, he looked a little bit prouder.

At the other side of the field, a big, worried shape was walking back and forth in front of a cave. It was Pedrão, Pebble's uncle! He had been looking for Pebble everywhere. When he saw the little one, his sleepy eyes opened wider than they had ever opened before.

Pebble ran to him as fast as his tiny legs could go, leaving a long line of little footprints behind him. Pedrão lowered his big head, and Pebble snuggled right under his chin. Pedrão closed his eyes, smiled, and then... Ah... ah... achoo! He sneezed so hard that all the snow fell off the trees, right on top of Tuca and Pipoca.

Everyone laughed, even Pedrão. And that afternoon, the four of them built the biggest snow dinosaur the valley had ever seen, with a pinecone nose and a little eggshell hat on top. Pebble added two tiny pebbles for its eyes, and Pedrão gave it a big round tail club, just like his own. Pipoca had started the day afraid of the snow. But by the end of it, Pipoca had learned to be brave, and had helped someone else be brave too.

That night, as the snow kept falling softly outside the cave, Pipoca fell asleep with a big smile. Because new things can feel scary at first... but when we try them with a friend, they can become the most wonderful adventures of all.
