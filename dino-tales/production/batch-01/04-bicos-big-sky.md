# Episode 4 — Bico's Big Sky
*O Grande Céu do Bico*

## A. Episode Information

- **Episode number:** 4
- **English title:** Bico's Big Sky
- **Portuguese title:** O Grande Céu do Bico
- **Main theme:** Self-confidence and the power of encouragement
- **Moral lesson:** When friends believe in us, it becomes easier to believe in ourselves.
- **Estimated duration:** 6:45 (12 scenes, narration 777 words)
- **Setting:** Sunny sea cliffs, a sandy beach with dunes and a turquoise lagoon, ending in a golden sunset sky festival
- **Characters:** Tuca, Pipoca, Bico, Nessa

**Synopsis:** Every young pteranodon flies through the Cloud Arch at the Sunset Sky Festival — but Bico has never flown higher than a palm tree. Tuca and Pipoca become his coaches: they practice on dunes, learn about the wind with a leaf kite and get a splashy lesson from Nessa. When Bico freezes at the edge of the cliff, it is his friends' silly cheering dance that reminds him he can do it.

## B. Complete Story and Narration

*(Single narrator. Ready for voice recording.)*

Once a year, when the sun turned the sky orange and pink, all the young pteranodons of the coast flew together through a giant arch made of clouds. It was called the Sunset Sky Festival. And this year, there was one little pteranodon who was very, very nervous about it.

That little pteranodon was Bico. Bico could flap. Bico could hop. Bico could even glide, a little. But every time he flew higher than a palm tree, his wings wobbled, his tummy flip-flopped, and down he went. Bonk! Right into the sand. Everyone said flying was easy. But for Bico, the sky looked very, very big. Tuca and Pipoca ran over to help him up.

Bico sat down, drooped his long beak, and looked at the faraway cliff where the festival would begin. All his friends would be there tonight. Tuca and Pipoca looked at each other. They could not fly. But they could help. And they had the whole day to do it.

First, they went to the sand dunes. Bico ran down the slope, spread his wings and glided a little farther each time. Pipoca ran beside him, flapping its tiny arms, which did not help it fly at all, but made everybody laugh. Tuca drew a line in the sand every time Bico went farther. By lunchtime, the lines in the sand stretched almost all the way to the water.

But Bico still could not go high. So Tuca made a kite out of a big palm leaf and a long vine. He ran across the beach, and the wind caught the kite and lifted it up, up, up into the sky. Bico watched with wide eyes. The kite was not flapping at all. It was simply resting on the wind. Maybe, Bico thought, flying was not about flapping harder. Maybe it was about trusting the wind.

Bico climbed onto a rock, spread his wings wide, and this time, he did not flap like crazy. He just waited for the breeze. Whoosh! The wind slipped under his wings and lifted him gently into the air, higher than before. But then he looked down... and splash!

Up from the water came Nessa, the friendly plesiosaur, with Bico sitting right on top of her head, dripping and embarrassed. Nessa giggled. Then she showed Bico something. She lifted her long neck high and looked straight ahead at the horizon, never down. Bico understood. When you fly, you look where you want to go. Bico tried it right away. He looked at a fluffy cloud far ahead, kept his eyes on it, and glided all the way across the lagoon without looking down even once.

Soon, the sky turned orange and pink. The festival was beginning. Young pteranodons lined up along the edge of the tall cliff, and one by one, they jumped and soared toward the Cloud Arch. Then it was Bico's turn. He walked to the edge, looked at the giant sky... and froze. The wind whistled, the sea looked very far below, and his wings felt as heavy as stones.

Down on the beach, Tuca and Pipoca saw him. They could not fly up there, but they could still help. So they started to dance. The silliest, wiggliest wing dance they had practiced all day, flapping their arms, spinning around and stomping their feet in the sand. It was the dance they had invented on the dunes that morning, the one that always made Bico laugh so hard that he forgot to be afraid.

Bico saw his friends dancing and laughed, and just like that, his tummy stopped flip-flopping. He remembered the leaf kite, resting on the wind. He remembered Nessa, looking straight ahead. Bico took a deep breath, spread his wings wide... and stepped off the cliff.

The wind caught him. Bico did not fall. He soared! Higher than the palm trees, higher than the cliff, all the way up to the glowing Cloud Arch. He flew right through the middle of it, leaving a swirl of pink cloud behind him. For the first time, the sky did not feel too big at all. It felt like it was made just for him. Below, Tuca and Pipoca jumped and cheered, and Nessa splashed so hard that she made a rainbow in the spray.

When Bico landed on the beach, a little wobbly but very proud, he wrapped his big wings around Tuca and Pipoca in the biggest hug of the evening. He had flown through the Cloud Arch. But he knew he had not done it alone. Because when friends believe in us, it becomes so much easier to believe in ourselves.
